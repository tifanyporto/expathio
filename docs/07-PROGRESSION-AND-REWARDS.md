# Expathio — Progression and Rewards

**Status:** IN DISCUSSION  
**Document type:** Progression and rewards specification  
**Project:** Expathio

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the career progression, performance evaluation, recognition, and reward systems within Expathio.

The objective is to make professional development a meaningful part of the participant experience.

Participants should be able to build a career inside an organization, accumulating experience, developing skills, earning recognition, receiving increased responsibilities, and progressing through professional positions.

Progression should reflect both the time spent in the organization and the quality and impact of the participant's contributions.

The system should feel like a professional career, not a conventional game leveling system.

## 2. Core Principles

The progression system must follow these principles. The project-wide design principles are in `00-VISION.md`, section 8; the list below applies them to progression.

- **Professional realism:** Career progression should resemble how professional growth happens in real organizations.
- **Experience and performance:** Both accumulated experience and demonstrated performance should influence progression.
- **Meaningful recognition:** Achievements should reflect actual contributions rather than activity volume.
- **Persistent career:** Professional history should remain available over time.
- **Organizational context:** Promotions and rewards must respect the company's structure, resources, and opportunities.
- **Fair evaluation:** Participants should understand how their work is evaluated.
- **Progressive responsibility:** Career advancement should introduce new responsibilities and expectations.
- **Multiple paths:** Professional development should not depend exclusively on management positions.
- **Long-term motivation:** Participants should have reasons to remain engaged beyond completing individual tasks.

## 3. Career Structure

Each organization should have a professional structure with positions and possible career paths.

A participant begins in a specific role and may progress through increasingly complex responsibilities.

A simplified example:

- Junior Analyst.
- Analyst.
- Senior Analyst.
- Specialist or Coordinator.
- Manager.
- Department Director.

This structure is illustrative.

Different departments should have different career paths.

A technology professional, marketing specialist, financial analyst, and operations coordinator should not necessarily follow identical progression structures.

### 3.1. Individual Contributor and Management Paths

Career advancement should not require every participant to become a manager.

Some professionals may prefer to deepen their technical or specialist expertise.

Possible career paths include:

- **Individual Contributor:** Progression through increasingly complex professional responsibilities and expertise.
- **Management:** Progression through coordination, leadership, and organizational decision-making.
- **Specialist:** Progression through advanced knowledge and recognized expertise in a specific area.

The organization should eventually support different professional trajectories.

## 4. Experience Accumulation

Participants should accumulate professional experience through their participation in the organization.

Experience may consider:

- Time spent in the organization.
- Time spent in the current position.
- Completed responsibilities.
- Exposure to different business situations.
- Participation in projects.
- Collaboration with other departments.
- Demonstrated professional development.
- Increasing complexity of assigned work.

Experience should not be equivalent to the number of tasks completed.

A participant who completes many simple activities should not automatically progress faster than someone who handles fewer but more complex responsibilities.

### 4.1. Experience and Time

Time should matter because professional growth requires continuity and accumulated exposure.

However, simply remaining connected to the platform should not generate unlimited progression.

Experience should be associated with meaningful participation and exposure to relevant professional activities.

The exact relationship between simulation time, real-world time, and career experience remains open.

## 5. Performance Evaluation

Performance should be evaluated according to the participant's responsibilities and actual contributions.

Potential evaluation dimensions include:

### 5.1. Quality

- Accuracy of deliverables.
- Compliance with requirements.
- Reliability of completed work.
- Attention to detail.
- Consistency of results.

### 5.2. Execution

- Ability to meet deadlines.
- Prioritization.
- Ownership of responsibilities.
- Effective use of available resources.
- Ability to handle complex activities.

### 5.3. Impact

- Contribution to business objectives.
- Improvement of organizational processes.
- Resolution of relevant problems.
- Positive effects on other departments.
- Measurable business outcomes when applicable.

### 5.4. Collaboration

- Communication quality.
- Cooperation with coworkers.
- Knowledge sharing.
- Coordination across departments.
- Contribution to collective objectives.

### 5.5. Professional Growth

- Improvement over time.
- Ability to handle greater complexity.
- Adaptability.
- Initiative.
- Development of relevant skills.
- Increasing autonomy.

Evaluation criteria should be adapted to the participant's role.

A customer service professional should not be evaluated using exactly the same criteria as a financial analyst or software developer.

## 6. Combined Progression Model

Expathio should combine experience-based and performance-based progression.

Neither should be sufficient on its own.

### Experience-Based Progression

Experience establishes professional maturity and accumulated exposure.

It helps determine whether a participant has spent enough time developing within a role to be considered for advancement.

### Performance-Based Progression

Performance demonstrates the participant's ability to handle responsibilities and produce meaningful results.

It helps determine whether the participant is ready for greater responsibility.

### Combined Evaluation

A potential promotion may consider:

- Minimum experience in the current position.
- Recent performance.
- Consistency over time.
- Complexity of completed responsibilities.
- Professional development.
- Organizational needs.
- Availability of an appropriate position.

The system should avoid automatic promotions based exclusively on reaching a numerical threshold.

## 7. Performance Reviews

Organizations should periodically evaluate participant performance. Formal reviews are not part of the initial release.

Reviews may be conducted by manager agents or other authorized organizational processes.

A review should provide:

- Evaluation period.
- Responsibilities considered.
- Relevant achievements.
- Areas requiring improvement.
- Examples of observed performance.
- Strengths.
- Development opportunities.
- Recommendations.
- Potential career implications.

Feedback should be specific and contextual.

Generic statements such as "good job" or "needs improvement" are insufficient without explaining the reasons.

Performance reviews should also preserve historical context so participants can understand how their professional trajectory evolved.

## 8. Promotions

Promotions represent meaningful changes in professional status and responsibility.

A promotion may involve:

- New professional title.
- Higher salary.
- Increased authority.
- More complex responsibilities.
- Greater autonomy.
- Leadership opportunities.
- Access to additional organizational information.
- Eligibility for different activities.

Promotions should not happen automatically whenever a participant completes enough tasks.

The organization must consider whether the advancement makes sense.

Potential factors include:

- Performance history.
- Experience.
- Professional readiness.
- Available positions.
- Departmental needs.
- Organizational structure.
- Management evaluation.

A participant may be ready for promotion but need to wait for an appropriate opportunity.

The system should communicate these situations clearly.

## 9. Fictional Compensation

Participants may receive fictional salaries within their organizations.

Compensation should reinforce the feeling of being part of a real company.

Possible compensation components include:

- Base salary.
- Salary increases.
- Performance bonuses.
- Special rewards.
- Project-based incentives.
- Profit-sharing mechanisms, when appropriate.

Performance bonuses and profit-sharing are long-term goals and are not part of the initial release.

Compensation should be associated with the participant's position, responsibilities, experience, and organizational context.

Salary changes should be recorded in the participant's professional history.

### 9.1. Compensation Changes

Salary increases may occur because of:

- Promotions.
- Periodic salary reviews.
- Strong and consistent performance.
- Increased responsibilities.
- Organizational compensation policies.

The system should avoid arbitrary compensation changes without an identifiable reason.

### 9.2. Fictional Financial Identity

Fictional compensation is part of the simulated organization.

It should not be confused with real-world money, actual employment, or guaranteed financial benefits.

The platform should communicate this distinction clearly.

## 10. Recognition and Rewards

Recognition should reinforce meaningful contributions.

Possible mechanisms include:

- Public recognition.
- Manager acknowledgments.
- Professional achievements.
- Special assignments.
- Invitations to important projects.
- Bonuses.
- Increased autonomy.
- New responsibilities.
- Career milestones.

Recognition should not be limited to numerical scores or badges.

A participant who identifies a serious operational problem, helps another department, or improves a business process may deserve recognition even if the contribution does not directly increase sales.

The system should reward behaviors that support the organization's long-term health.

## 11. Achievements and Milestones

Achievements may represent meaningful professional milestones.

Examples include:

- Completing an important project.
- Successfully handling a complex incident.
- Improving an operational process.
- Resolving a significant customer problem.
- Leading a cross-department initiative.
- Demonstrating consistent performance.
- Reaching a professional milestone.
- Receiving a promotion.

Achievements should be tied to identifiable events or contributions.

They should represent evidence of professional activity rather than arbitrary engagement rewards.

## 12. Responsibility and Autonomy Progression

Career advancement should change how participants interact with the organization.

As participants develop, they may receive:

- More complex assignments.
- Greater decision-making authority.
- Less supervision.
- Responsibility for projects.
- Permission to approve certain operations.
- Opportunities to coordinate coworkers.
- Participation in strategic decisions.

Increased autonomy must be accompanied by appropriate permissions and accountability.

The system should not grant sensitive organizational privileges solely because a participant has accumulated experience points.

## 13. Career Opportunities

The organization should be capable of presenting opportunities for professional growth.

Examples include:

- Internal job openings.
- Department transfers.
- Special projects.
- Temporary leadership assignments.
- Specialist positions.
- New areas of responsibility.

Opportunities should emerge from organizational circumstances rather than appearing exclusively as predetermined rewards.

Participants should be able to understand the requirements and implications of pursuing a new opportunity.

## 14. Career History

The platform should maintain a persistent record of professional development.

This history may include:

- Previous positions.
- Departments.
- Time in each position.
- Promotions.
- Salary changes.
- Performance reviews.
- Achievements.
- Important projects.
- Recognition.
- Major contributions.
- Responsibility changes.

Career history should help participants understand their professional evolution and provide evidence of their contributions within Expathio.

## 15. Fairness and Evaluation Integrity

The progression system must avoid rewarding superficial behavior.

Potential problems include:

- Completing many trivial tasks to accumulate experience.
- Repeatedly performing the same activity to inflate performance.
- Prioritizing individual metrics over organizational outcomes.
- Manipulating workflows to obtain rewards.
- Favoring participants who are continuously online.
- Penalizing participants for events outside their control.
- Rewarding speed while ignoring quality.

Evaluation should consider context, responsibility, difficulty, and actual contribution.

The system should distinguish between participant performance and external circumstances affecting results.

## 16. AI-Assisted Evaluation

AI may assist managers in evaluating professional contributions.

Possible uses include:

- Summarizing completed work.
- Identifying patterns in performance.
- Comparing deliverables against requirements.
- Preparing contextual feedback.
- Identifying development opportunities.
- Supporting promotion recommendations.

AI should not independently determine every important career outcome.

High-impact decisions should be based on explicit criteria, relevant organizational information, and appropriate validation.

Evaluation records should preserve the reasons behind important decisions.

## 17. Relationship with Organizational Economics

Career progression should be connected to the simulated company's economic reality.

Promotions, salary increases, bonuses, and rewards should consider the organization's financial situation and policies.

The system should avoid treating fictional compensation as an unlimited reward resource.

Organizational growth, financial constraints, and departmental budgets may influence available opportunities.

This connection should be introduced progressively, beginning with a simplified but coherent compensation model.

## 18. Open Questions

The following decisions remain open:

- How should experience be measured?
- How should simulation time influence career progression?
- How frequently should performance reviews occur?
- Which criteria should apply to each professional role?
- How should performance and experience be weighted?
- How should promotions work when no position is available?
- Should participants be able to negotiate compensation?
- How should bonuses and incentives be calculated?
- How should different career paths interact?
- How should the system prevent progression manipulation?
- How much influence should AI managers have over career decisions?
- How should career progression relate to organizational financial performance?

These decisions must be resolved before Phase 7 starts (`docs/06-ROADMAP.md`, section 10). They should be validated through the initial organization and refined as the simulation becomes more sophisticated.

## 19. Guiding Principle

A participant should feel that they are building a professional career within a living organization.

Progression should emerge from accumulated experience, demonstrated performance, meaningful contributions, and organizational opportunities.

The goal is not to make participants chase points.

The goal is to make them care about the work they perform, the reputation they build, the responsibilities they earn, and the future they can create within the company.
